The holidays are a moment when the world slows down for a while.

For many people, it is a time of joy and gathering, but I know that for those who struggle with pain, it can also be filled with conflicting emotions.

Because pain doesn't take a holiday break. It can be quiet, persistent, sometimes sudden — and often invisible to others.

Today, I want to say one thing especially clearly:

**Your experience matters.**

Regardless of whether pain has been with you for years or you're only now learning to live with it, you have the right to feel exhausted, to have bad days, to have moments of weakness.

You also have the right to hope — even when it seems very far away.

The holidays don't have to be "perfect." They don't have to look like a postcard. It's enough that they are yours. That might mean a quieter day, shorter gatherings, more silence, and fewer expectations. It might mean accepting that today you're doing only as much as you truly can. And that is perfectly fine.

I wish that during this holiday time:

- There is space to breathe — without tension and pressure
- Someone genuinely asks "how are you feeling?" and truly listens
- There are moments of relief — even brief, but real
- And in your heart, a thought takes root that tomorrow might be a little easier than today

Remember that strength doesn't always look like a fight. Sometimes strength is pausing, resting, asking for help, or simply making it through another day. Every step you take — even the smallest one — matters.

For the holidays ahead, I wish you gentleness toward yourself, more understanding — from others and from yourself — and hope that doesn't shout but endures.

You are not alone. You matter. And you deserve peace.

Warm, peaceful holidays to you.
