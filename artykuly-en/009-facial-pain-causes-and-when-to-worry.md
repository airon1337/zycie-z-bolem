Facial pain can have many causes — from dental problems to simple tooth sensitivity. However, sometimes its source is trigeminal neuralgia, a condition involving the nervous system. Early on, its symptoms are easily confused with other complaints, which is why it is important to understand what this pain looks like and how it differs from typical dental or sinus issues.

## Classical (Paroxysmal) Trigeminal Neuralgia

- The hallmark is sudden, piercing pain, often described as an "electric shock," a "bolt," or a "spark of pain."
- Attacks last from a fraction of a second to a few minutes and may recur many times throughout the day.
- Minor stimuli are enough to trigger an attack — brushing teeth, chewing, speaking, touching the skin, or even a gust of wind.
- Between attacks, there is typically no pain at all — the patient feels completely normal.

## Trigeminal Neuralgia / Neuropathy with Constant Pain

- Instead of (or in addition to) paroxysmal "bolts of pain," there is continuous, chronic facial pain — often described as burning, pressure-like, dull, or stabbing.
- It may be accompanied by sensory symptoms: paresthesias (tingling, numbness), skin hypersensitivity, a feeling of swelling.
- Pain intensity fluctuates throughout the day but never disappears completely, giving the condition a chronic character.
- In some patients, paroxysmal attacks still occur but are "superimposed" on a constant background of pain, making daily functioning exceptionally difficult.

## When to Be Concerned

- If facial pain is disproportionately severe relative to the triggering stimulus.
- When it recurs repeatedly during ordinary activities (speaking, eating, washing the face).
- If continuous facial pain persists despite dental or ENT treatment that provides no relief.

## Why This Matters

Early recognition of trigeminal neuralgia or trigeminal neuropathy allows appropriate pharmacological or interventional treatment to be initiated, reducing the risk of progression to a chronic form that is more difficult to manage.

If you notice these symptoms in yourself or someone close to you, it is worth consulting not only a dentist but also a neurologist and a pain specialist who specialize in diagnosing and treating this condition.
