## What trigeminal neuralgia feels like

"Imagine your face being struck by something like a lightning bolt made of knives. Without warning, without logic. Sudden, pure, unimaginable pain. That is what trigeminal neuralgia feels like — also called 'the suicide disease.' And unfortunately, that name didn't come from nowhere.

This condition affects the trigeminal nerve, an extremely important facial nerve responsible for chewing, blinking, and sensation in the cheeks, among other things. But when it starts malfunctioning, it can feel as though someone is shocking your face with an exceptionally sadistic stun gun.

Everyday activities like brushing your teeth, speaking, or even a gust of wind on your skin can trigger instant maximum-intensity pain. And this isn't a simple 'ouch, my head hurts.' It's more like someone just drove an ice pick made of electricity into your jaw.

The pain can appear in sudden, exceptionally violent attacks, or persist for longer periods like a guest who visits and absolutely refuses to leave.

What makes it worse is that trigeminal neuralgia isn't always visible on imaging studies. Because of this, some patients are misdiagnosed, dismissed, or told they're exaggerating.

Doctors still aren't 100% certain why this condition occurs. The cause may be a blood vessel compressing the nerve, or other disturbances in nervous system function.

Medications can help alleviate symptoms, but in some cases surgery is necessary to calm this storm of pain.

Trigeminal neuralgia — one of the most painful conditions known to medicine."
