The New Year often arrives with a ready-made script: resolutions, goals, changes.

Except not everyone has the ability to enter the New Year in the same way.

And it's not a matter of attitude or lack of motivation.

It's simply the reality that some people face every day.

That's why, instead of promises of breakthroughs, I want to pause at what truly helps you function when daily life is demanding.

So I wish you:

**Moments of greater calm in your nervous system.**

Those moments when your body, even briefly, steps out of constant readiness mode. A few calmer breaths. A few moments of less tension.

**Regulation that's slightly better than yesterday.**

Not perfect. Not constant. Just a little more effective — because real change is built from small, repeatable differences.

**Trust in yourself and in your own experience.**

Without judgment. Without guilt. With the recognition that what you are going through is real and it matters.

**A sense of influence, even if it's limited.**

Because not everything can be controlled, but many things can be consciously shaped: the rhythm of your day, your pace, rest, boundaries, the way you treat yourself.

Today we know that the nervous system does not return to balance under pressure. It needs conditions that support stabilization: safety, predictability, and cooperation — not constant mobilization.

This year can be a time of learning to live with pain in a way that doesn't consume all the space for being yourself.

Of building a daily life where pain is one element of the experience, not the only point of reference.

I wish you:

- Specialists who listen and take you seriously
- People around whom you don't have to explain yourself
- Less internal struggle
- More gentleness toward yourself

Your worth does not depend on how well you endure pain.

You don't have to be brave all the time.

It's enough that you are here.

A peaceful New Year.

One that supports — instead of demands.
