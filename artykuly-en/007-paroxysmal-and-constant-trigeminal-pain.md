## Paroxysmal (Typical) Pain

These are sudden, brief strikes of pain resembling an electric shock. They last from a fraction of a second to a few minutes, but their intensity can be paralyzing and rob you of the ability to function normally. Attacks may occur individually or in clusters — even dozens of times in a single day.

They are often triggered by completely ordinary activities, such as:

- brushing teeth,
- speaking,
- chewing food,
- touching the face,
- or even a gust of wind or a change in temperature.

This is what makes life with this form of pain a state of constant tension and fear of the next attack — because you never know what will trigger it or when it will come.

## Constant (Atypical) Pain

Less well known, but equally devastating. This is a continuous, deep, burning or throbbing sensation that can persist for hours, days, or even weeks. Although usually less intense than paroxysmal pain, it is present almost without interruption and often intensifies suddenly, escalating into severe attacks.

This constant pain:

- impairs concentration,
- causes chronic fatigue,
- leads to physical and psychological exhaustion.

Many patients describe it as "unrelenting torture," radiating across the face, jaw, and even the neck. It is particularly difficult to control and invisible to those around you — which leaves patients feeling misunderstood and profoundly alone in their suffering.
