Nerve blocks in trigeminal neuralgia are a quick and often effective method of pain relief. Although they do not work for everyone and are not a definitive solution, for many patients they bring a significant improvement in quality of life — especially when medications are insufficient or cause troublesome side effects. The procedure can provide relief after the very first treatment, and sometimes allows pain-free functioning for weeks or months.

## What Is a Nerve Block?

A nerve block involves injecting a specialized preparation near the nerve — an anesthetic, often combined with a corticosteroid.

- The anesthetic numbs the nerve and interrupts pain conduction.
- The corticosteroid reduces inflammation and extends the duration of the block.

The procedure is performed with a thin needle, usually under ultrasound guidance, to ensure precise delivery of the medication.

## Which Nerves Can Be Blocked?

The trigeminal nerve has three branches — depending on the location of pain, different sites are targeted:

- **Ophthalmic branch (V1)** — supraorbital and supratrochlear nerve blocks (forehead and periorbital pain).
- **Maxillary branch (V2)** — infraorbital nerve block (cheek and upper jaw pain).
- **Mandibular branch (V3)** — mental, inferior alveolar, and lingual nerve blocks (jaw, chin, and lower teeth pain).

In classical trigeminal neuralgia, blocks may also be performed closer to where the nerve exits the skull.

## When Are Blocks Considered?

Nerve blocks are performed when:

- pain attacks are very severe and do not respond to medication,
- the patient cannot take oral medications (e.g., during pregnancy, or with kidney, gastric, or liver disease),
- postherpetic neuralgia, migraines, or other facial pain conditions are present,
- there is a need to rapidly interrupt an exceptionally severe pain episode.

## What Are the Results?

- In many patients, pain resolves immediately.
- The effect may last from a few days to several months, sometimes even longer.
- The need for oral medications is reduced.
- After the procedure, patients typically function normally — they can return to work or drive.

## Is It Safe?

Nerve blocks are a minimally invasive and relatively safe method.

Possible but rare side effects include:

- hematoma or bruising at the injection site,
- temporary skin numbness.

Serious complications are extremely rare, particularly when the procedure is performed by an experienced physician under ultrasound guidance.

## Limitations and Next Steps

- Blocks do not always work and do not always provide lasting relief.
- In some patients, the procedure needs to be repeated.
- If relief is short-lived, the physician may suggest alternative methods:
  - cryoablation (freezing the nerve),
  - radiofrequency thermoablation (heating the nerve with radiofrequency waves),
  - neurosurgical procedures, such as CyberKnife or Gamma Knife.
