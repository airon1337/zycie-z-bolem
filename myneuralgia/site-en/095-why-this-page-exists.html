Why This Page Exists

Author: Natalia · Date: 2026-02-26 · 2 min read
Category: Living with Pain

"It looks like you're profiting from our suffering. Can't you be more original? I'll report you to admin if I see this again."

That comment deeply saddened me today.

This page about pain was not created for profit. It was born from a need for education, support, and a space with reliable, verified knowledge — knowledge that I check, analyze, and filter through experience and available medical sources.

Twelve years ago, when my trigeminal nerve was damaged, places like this didn't exist. I was surrounded by the support of loved ones, and yet I felt terribly alone. Because if someone has not experienced trigeminal nerve damage, has not lived with neuropathic pain or classical neuralgia, they cannot fully understand what daily life in a world of pain looks like.

This page was created precisely so that people living with facial pain, neuropathic pain, or neuralgia can find not only knowledge but also tools for working with pain — especially when available medical methods don't bring the expected results.

I made a promise to myself: I will verify every advertised medication, supplement, or "miracle therapy" for neuralgia that is not used in medical practice. So that no one ever profits from our suffering. So that expensive, magical treatments without proven efficacy do not find space for promotion here.

This place was created for people who seek calm, understanding, and reliable information.

The page doesn't have a formal code of conduct yet, but I ask: let's respect our health, let's respect one another, and let's create a space free from hostility.

I'm here with you too — on the other side of the mirror. In the world of pain, medical surrealism, and the daily fight to get through.

And I promise: if a specific supplement, medication, or therapy is ever recommended here, it will be preceded by thorough analysis, verified sources, and genuine hope of help — not marketing.

This place is about dignity. About knowledge. About support. And about honesty.

⚠️ This content is for educational purposes only and does not replace medical consultation, diagnosis, or treatment. If you experience severe pain or concerning symptoms, contact your doctor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is trigeminal neuralgia?

Trigeminal neuralgia is a chronic facial pain condition. It manifests as sudden, severe, usually one-sided pain attacks — often described as an electric shock. A single attack typically lasts from a few seconds to about two minutes.

What is the difference between neuralgia and neuropathy?

In simple terms: neuralgia primarily involves paroxysmal pain along a nerve, usually without loss of sensation. Neuropathy involves nerve damage that more often causes constant pain along with numbness and sensory loss.

Do regular painkillers help with neuropathic pain?

Usually not. Neuropathic pain responds poorly to paracetamol or anti-inflammatory drugs. Instead, medications that act on nerves are used, such as anticonvulsants like carbamazepine.

Can trigeminal neuralgia be cured?

In many people, pain can be effectively controlled with medications or procedures, and MVD provides the most durable results in eligible patients. The course varies — with periods of remission and relapse. Decisions are made together with your doctor.
