Pain plays an important protective role under normal circumstances. It warns us of injury and encourages us to protect our bodies. In most cases, it subsides as damaged tissues heal.

However, this is not always the case. Sometimes pain persists even though its original cause has already resolved. This is what we refer to as **chronic pain**.

Chronic pain is defined as pain that lasts for more than three months. Over time, it affects not only the body but also mental well-being. It can limit daily activities, lead to social isolation, lower mood, and even contribute to symptoms of depression. Moreover, physical weakness and negative emotions can themselves further intensify the perception of pain.

## Types of Chronic Pain

**Nociceptive pain** originates in body tissues, such as muscles, bones, or internal organs.

**Neuropathic pain** results from damage to the nervous system and is commonly described as stabbing, burning, or electric shock-like.

Neuropathic pain is particularly familiar to people living with trigeminal neuralgia, one of the most severe and persistent forms of facial pain. Pain episodes may occur suddenly or present as continuous, ongoing pain. They can feel like electric shocks, be extremely intense, and significantly affect everyday life.
