PAIN DIARY

A tool for monitoring, understanding, and treating chronic pain

Why keep a diary? It helps you better understand your condition, notice recurring patterns, and identify what makes symptoms worse or better. Regular entries also make it easier to assess treatment effectiveness and prepare for appointments with your doctor. This gives your doctor a more structured picture of your disease progression. Consistency is key.

Guidelines and Pain Scale

Record at consistent times: Ideally in the morning, during the day, and in the evening. If an unusually severe pain attack occurs, record it separately. Pay attention to factors such as: sleep, stress, fatigue, cold, wind, weather, physical effort, eating, or talking.

LevelPain intensity description (Scale 0–4)
0No pain – complete comfort.
1Mild pain – noticeable but does not interfere with daily activities.
2Moderate pain – clearly felt, impairs concentration or some activities.
3Severe pain – very distressing, dominates attention, significantly limits functioning.
4Unbearable pain – prevents any activity, requires urgent intervention.

Why a 0–4 scale? Unlike the traditional 0–10 scale, the five-level Verbal Rating Scale (VRS) significantly reduces subjectivity and eliminates the patient's dilemma of choosing a specific number. It is a clinically validated approach that allows for quick, intuitive, and reproducible pain assessment – which is crucial for daily, repeated diary entries during states of severe fatigue or symptom exhaustion.

About the author

Hi, my name is Natalia. Since 2014, I have been living with chronic pain caused by trigeminal nerve damage. My story began suddenly. I was barely 25 when nerve damage occurred after surgery to remove a tumor. Overnight, severe pain appeared in my jaw area – and it has stayed with me since. To understand it, I began studying pain in depth, and I want to share that knowledge.
